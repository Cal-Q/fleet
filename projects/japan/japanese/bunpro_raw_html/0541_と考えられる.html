About と考えられる

In order to express an objective opinion in Japanese, the expression と考(かんが)えられる will often be used. This is a combination of と, and the passive-indicating auxiliary verb られる. Common translations for this structure include 'can be considered as (A)', 'is thought to be (A)', or 'it is conceivable that (A)'.

As と考(かんが)えられる begins with と, this structure will frequently appear at the end of entire phrases, converting them into a quote.

When wanting to express a subjective opinion, と思(おも)われる will be used instead.

Caution

Compared to と考(かんが)えられている, と考(かんが)えられる focuses more on judgements, while the former is primarily used to illustrate widely accepted opinions.

Fun-fact

The primary difference between a subjective and objective opinion is that an objective opinion is primarily based on observable facts about the topic (unbiased and focuses on the object), while a subjective opinion is based more on emotions, feelings, or preferences (may be biased, regardless of what is actually true about the topic).

Examples

--:--

    衆議院(しゅうぎいん)、いよいよ解散(かいさん)すると考(かんが)えられる。

    It is thought that the lower house of representatives will finally dissolve.

    あの子(こ)の親(おや)は育児(いくじ)放棄(ほうき)していると考(かんが)えられる。

    It is thought that that child's parents are neglecting the child.

    あの政策(せいさく)なら、一定(いってい)の支持(しじ)が得(え)られると考(かんが)えられる。

    If it is that policy, it is conceivable that we would receive standardized support.

    安易(あんい)に決定(けってい)したと考(かんが)えられるが、もしかしたら作戦(さくせん)かもしれません。

    It is thought that the decision made was to be easy-going, but it might just be a tactic.

    そのプロジェクト、一旦(いったん)白紙(はくし)に戻(もど)すということも考(かんが)えられませんか？

    Is it conceivable that you would think of wiping the slate clean on that project? (won't you?)
