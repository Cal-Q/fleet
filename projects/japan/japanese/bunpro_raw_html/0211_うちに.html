About うちに

When うち, (sometimes seen using the kanji 内(うち), or 裡(うち)) is paired with the case marking particle に, it expresses a period/space 'within' which something else will/should happen/exist. It quite often (but not always) carries the nuance of (A) being the 'best', or the 'optimal' time/space within which (B) could occur/exist. In English, うちに can simply be translated as 'while', or 'during'.

うちに may be paired with the dictionary or ている form of verbs, the plain form of い-Adjectives, nouns followed by の, or な-Adjectives that are followed by な.

Unlike 間(あいだ)に, うちに expresses something that can happen/exist anywhere 'within' the span of (A), and is therefore non-specific as to where (B) is located.

Fun Fact

The kanji 裡(うち) refers literally to the inner walls/lining of something, and anything that is enclosed within that space. This can be thought of as where the 'non-specificity' of うち comes from, in regard to where within that space something is.

Examples

--:--

    近(ちか)い内(うち)に連絡(れんらく)します。

    I'll be in touch within the near future.

    鉄(てつ)は熱(あつ)い内(うち)に打(う)て。

    Strike while the iron is hot.

    授業(じゅぎょう)を受(う)けている内(うち)に、宿題(しゅくだい)を完成(かんせい)させた。

    I was able to finish my homework while taking a class.

    赤(あか)ちゃんは食(た)べている内(うち)に寝(ね)てしまった。

    The baby fell asleep while eating.

    日本(にほん)にいる内(うち)に東京(とうきょう)に行(い)ってみたい。

    While I am in Japan, I want to see Tokyo.
